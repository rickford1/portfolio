Overview

I was the Lead Undergraduate Researcher in Dr. Rolf Müller's Bio-Inspired Science and Technology Lab at Virginia Tech. The lab studies how bats achieve highly capable mobility and sensing in dense natural environments, and translates that biology into biomimetic robotic platforms. My piece of the effort was building a soft-robotics rig that replicates the ear movements bats use during biosonar-based navigation.

Why Bats

Bats can navigate cluttered, unstructured environments like dense foliage and narrow passageways using biosonar echoes. A big piece of how they do this is active control of their ear morphology: roughly 20 muscles on each ear deform the shape of the pinna in real time, modulating how incoming echoes are received. Replicating that deformable structure in a robot is a prerequisite for studying how dynamic ear motion shapes sensing, and ultimately for engineered systems that can navigate the same kinds of environments.

The Rig

I helped design and manufacture a soft-robotics tendon actuation rig that mimics the ear deformation bats use during biosonar navigation. It was a full-team effort, I led the undergraduate side, working alongside graduate researchers and lab staff. The rig gave the lab a hardware platform to experimentally study how dynamic ear motion affects echo reception, feeding into the group's broader work on deep-learning-based interpretation of clutter echoes and autonomy in unstructured natural environments.

My Contributions

  • Led the undergraduate side of the rig effort, coordinating across team members from design through manufacture and integration.
  • Contributed to the mechanical design of the rig in CAD and managed the day-to-day build process.
  • Standardized the lab's manufacturing process for robotic components using SLA and FDM 3D printing, reducing iteration time on hardware revisions and making the workflow reproducible for new researchers.

Where the Research Goes

The lab's broader effort targets autonomous operation in complex natural environments, applications include precision agriculture and forestry, environmental monitoring, and navigation in GPS-denied settings.
